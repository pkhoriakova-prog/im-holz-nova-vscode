Our approach

Our Approach

Building better, together

No single technology makes a building sustainable. We aim to bring together materials, engineering, energy, water and local expertise in one process, drawing on Austrian timber construction.

  1. Lower embodied carbon

    Timber holds on to the carbon it has absorbed for as long as it stays in use in a building.

  2. Healthier indoor spaces

    Natural materials, daylight and good air for the people who use the building every day.

  3. Resource-efficient construction

    Prefabricated, dry-assembled elements are made off site and put together on site.

  4. Buildings that stay in use

    Planning schools, healthcare and public buildings for uses that can stay relevant to their communities.

Announcement

A House of Austrian Technologies for Vienna is taking shape

We are developing the concept for a demonstration centre in Vienna exploring Austrian approaches to sustainable development. Conceived as both a working space and a living showcase, it is meant to bring together materials, technologies and expertise across sectors — showing how Austrian solutions can work together in real-world environments.

The project is currently at an early stage, with its scope, partnerships and funding model being defined. Our ambition is to create more than a showroom: a place where technologies can be experienced in context, ideas can be tested and new collaborations can take shape — in Vienna and beyond.

Eco Solutions

Ten solutions, one way of building

The technologies we aim to bring together for sustainable building, each explained on its own page: how it is applied and what it can offer.

  1. CLT and glulam can form the load-bearing structure. Panels and frames are prefabricated off site and assembled on site.

    Read more

  2. Facade elements can be assembled dry and delivered in sequence, which can mean less work on site.

  3. Concrete can be kept to cores, stairs and foundations, with timber carrying the rest. The aim is to reduce embodied carbon while keeping the structure stable.

  4. Fire safety in timber buildings is addressed through design and detailing, protective layers and tested connections.

  5. Planting can be carried on the timber deck itself. A green roof can retain rainwater, help cool the building in summer and add habitat.

  6. Renewable energy can support low-carbon operation, with heating and solar generation matched to the building and its climate.

  7. Water treatment and reuse for public buildings, with supply and sanitation adapted to local conditions.

  8. Ventilation and daylight for the people inside. Natural materials and good air quality can support health, comfort and concentration.

  9. Dry connections: members can be bolted rather than cast in, so a frame can be taken apart and its parts reused.

  10. Trees, planted courtyards and green spaces can bring shade and nature closer to buildings. Planting adapted to local conditions can support biodiversity and help manage rainwater.

Standards & Certifications

The standards behind sustainable timber building

These are the frameworks that sustainable timber projects in Europe are commonly assessed against. We are building our expertise in them.

FSC / PEFC

Certified timber

Forest certification schemes that confirm timber comes from responsibly managed forests.

CE marking

CE-marked products

The EU marking for construction products placed on the European market.

EN 15978

Life-cycle assessment

The European standard for assessing the environmental performance of buildings over their life cycle.

EU Taxonomy

Green finance criteria

The EU classification of environmentally sustainable activities, used to screen green finance.
