A climate-resilient timber school for Central Asia, built to repeat

The CA Smart School concept is a replicable, low-carbon school model built with European mass timber, designed for Central Asian conditions and structured for co-financing. It is at the concept stage.

The challenge

The Challenge

Too few schools, and the ones that exist are crowded and ageing

Uzbekistan alone needs more than 1 000 new school buildings by 2030. Today six in ten existing schools teach in double shifts, so children get fewer hours in class. Most of the stock dates from the 1960s–1980s: concrete frames that use around three times the energy of an EU school, cost a lot to maintain and, in seismic zones, often fall short of today’s norms.

So the question is not only how many schools, but how they are built. New schools are needed fast, and they have to be safer, warmer and cheaper to run. Conventional concrete takes 24–36 months and carries 400–500 kg of CO₂ per m², and there is no regional precedent yet for public schools in mass timber. This pilot starts there.

School Concept

A school for 750 students,
built in timber around a courtyard

The pilot school is a U-shaped building that opens to the street and wraps around a protected courtyard of 26 × 19 m: a sheltered place for outdoor learning that stretches the season in a continental climate. The wings step from two to four storeys, so daylight reaches the yard and the building stays under 15 m.

Inside are classrooms, an IT and maker space, a library, art and music rooms, a cafeteria and a sports hall, and on the roof a 300 m² auditorium that the neighbourhood can use after school hours.

The frame is cross-laminated timber on glulam columns across a 6 × 6 m grid, left exposed in the classrooms. Concrete is limited to the ground slab, the lift shafts and the foundations. Passive House ventilation, a heat pump and rooftop solar aim at under 50 kWh/m² a year.

Why It Works

Why mass timber suits this school

Mass timber is not a compromise for a school. It answers the problems of the existing stock head on: the structure is prefabricated in a factory, so the building goes up fast even in a continental winter. It carries less carbon than concrete and costs less to run over its life.

The same grid and the same panels repeat from school to school, which is what makes the model replicable. Here are six reasons it holds up, in the order a client usually weighs them.

Engineered timber in detail

  1. CLT stores about 1 tCO₂ per m³. Embodied carbon is estimated at 45% below an equivalent reinforced-concrete frame: under 150 kg CO₂/m², against a 430 kg/m² baseline.

  2. Factory-prefabricated panels work through harsh continental winters. The structural frame is erected in 10–14 weeks against 24–30 for concrete, and the school can be ready within about 18 months.

  3. Exposed CLT ceilings replace suspended ceilings and bring a natural, calmer interior. Passive House ventilation keeps the air fresh while keeping energy use low.

  4. One 6 m structural grid and one panel specification repeat across every floor, so the design package can be transferred to 10 or more schools with little adaptation.

  5. The upfront cost is comparable to concrete, with an estimated premium of 5–8%. Over 50 years, lower energy and maintenance costs bring the total cost of ownership an estimated 15–22% below concrete.

  6. The concept builds on documented European school projects, among them Fachschule Rotholz and BORG Radstadt in Austria and Gymnasium Langenhagen in Germany, with CE-marked, FSC/PEFC-certified timber.
